CareLink home display

Tired of grabbing your phone to keep an eye on diabetes? Reading a glucose value should be as easy as reading the clock.

People living with diabetes look at their glucose more often than at the time. Now imagine unlocking your phone, opening an app, waiting, and tapping a banner, just to know what time it is. This small e-paper screen hangs in the kitchen or the hallway like a clock and shows what the insulin pump is doing: the glucose reading, its trend, active insulin and any pump alarm, with a beep. For the person with diabetes, and for the parents, partners and grandparents around them.

How the numbers get there every five minutes, one way only

Five screens, one switch M5Stack Core Ink, 1.54" e-paper, buzzer; 4–5 days on one charge

The side switch on the right edge is the only control in daily use: flick it down for the next screen, up for the previous one. The buzzer beeps when the pump raises an alarm, and again every five minutes while the alarm shows. The screen keeps its last picture even without power, so a stopped display could look like a working one. That is why it shows the time of the reading, and when readings stop for about 15 minutes, a banner says why: no sensor data, no link to the box, or a CareLink login needed.

On the move

The box and the display travel together. The box joins the hotel WiFi or a phone hotspot and runs its own small hotspot, which the display finds by itself. What you see at this stand: the box on a power bank, in travel mode.

What it is not

  • Not a medical device. The pump and the patient's phone stay the alarms anyone acts on.
  • Read-only: nothing in the home can change anything on the pump.
  • The display never talks to Medtronic; one box holds the one CareLink login.
